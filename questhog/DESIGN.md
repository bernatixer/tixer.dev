# QuestHog — design notes

## Four versions before this one

Each was abandoned for a reason worth keeping.

1. **An on-call shift at a shop**, with tickets and a trace viewer. Accurate, and
   nobody wanted to play it.
2. **A fantasy village** where four spirits did an oracle's thinking and you
   accused one. Prettier, still a quiz, and the wording was fussy.
3. **The same village, one continuous day.** Better pacing, same problem: the
   verb was "pick the guilty one from a list".
4. **A farm** where a magical farmhand worked badly and you read its log. Closer,
   because reading the log was the only way through. But it was still a debugging
   exercise, the player was told outright that the farmhand was an AI, and after
   five minutes it had taught nothing.

What was wrong every time: **the player was being examined.** Observability was
the subject of a test rather than something you reach for because you want
something.

## What this one does

You are not debugging anything. You want to catch a boat. You ask four people,
you pick one, and you leave. The game is over before it teaches you anything.

Then it turns:

- **You cannot remember what they said.** That is true of the player and true of
  anyone running an AI product. It is the actual problem, felt rather than
  described.
- **The notebook was being kept anyway.** Not a feature you switched on. Not a
  reward. It was just there, because things that write themselves down are worth
  more than things you have to remember to record.
- **You write the rule, and something else does the reading.** Four
  conversations is already too many to hold in your head. That is an eval, and
  it is the only honest way to show why one is needed.
- **The winner changes with the question.** This is the note the whole thing
  hangs on. Kip invented his directions and still tops the board when you ask
  "did they answer?". The eval is not broken; it did exactly what you asked. Bad
  evals are the most common way teams fool themselves, and it lands here in about
  ten seconds.

Only at the very end does anything say the word AI.

## Rules the design follows

- **No prompt box.** The player never types at a model. Every call happens
  because a character in the world had a reason to speak.
- **The four are genuinely different models calls**, not a lookup table, so a
  second playthrough is not the same text.
- **The judge is a real call too.** The scores come from a model reading the
  transcripts against your rule. Faking it would make the demo a lie.
- **Nothing is called a trace until the end.** It is a notebook, ruled, with a
  tab per person. Same data, no jargon.
- **The prompt is never ambiguous.** Standing near someone shows a filled green
  "press space" chip and what it will do. An earlier version left people
  wondering whether to press anything at all.
- **Art and music are code.** Scenery is generated so its shading is consistent,
  townsfolk are one body recoloured, and the chiptune is Web Audio. Nothing to
  license, nothing to download, and the whole game stays a static site.

## About buying art in

The art here is generated by `scripts/gen-sprites.py` rather than downloaded,
which keeps the repo free of third-party licences and the build free of binary
assets. If you would rather drop in a real tileset, these are the ones worth
looking at, all usable commercially:

- [Free Top-Down RPG 32x32 Tile Set by Mixel](https://mixelslime.itch.io/free-top-down-rpg-32x32-tile-set) — grass, connecting paths, trees, rocks, mushrooms, at exactly the size this game already uses.
- [Grasslands Free by Lamora](https://lamorapedia.itch.io/grasslands-free) — 32x32 grass, dirt and cobble paths, trees, fences and signs.
- [Peaceful Forest by Snakerser](https://snakerser.itch.io/peacefulforest) — closest to the soft look in the reference shot.
- [OpenGameArt with the CC0 filter on](https://opengameart.org/content/good-cc0-art) — the safest source when licence matters more than looks.

Whichever you pick, the townsfolk should stay hand-drawn hogs. They are the one
thing that has to be PostHog's, and no pack will have them.

## Not built yet

- Writing your own criterion in free text, rather than picking one of four. The
  judge already takes an arbitrary rule, so this is a text input and nothing more.
- Walk cycles with direction. The hogs currently face the camera and shuffle
  their feet; facing the way you walk would cost four more sprites each.
- A second town, or any reason to play twice beyond seeing different answers.
- **Datasets** — freeze these four conversations and re-run a changed prompt
  against them.
- **Clustering** — with four conversations you read them all. With forty thousand
  you cannot, and that is the next thing PostHog gives you.

## Deploying

Not wired up yet. The intended shape matches `focus/`: a Cloudflare Pages
project plus a `.github/workflows/deploy-questhog.yml` that builds and runs
`wrangler pages deploy dist`. That needs the Pages project to exist first, so it
is a deliberate manual step rather than a workflow that would fail on its first
push.

Nothing here needs the worker or D1. The game is entirely client side.
